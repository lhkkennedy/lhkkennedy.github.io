01 / Abstract

The experiment asks whether the route by which displayed liquidity rebuilds after a depletion shock contains genuinely future information after the final Level-1 book state is already known.

On a fixed one-day ETHUSDT benchmark, adding recovery-path features to endpoint queue imbalance, spread and early price movement lowers out-of-sample log loss and Brier score in all four chronological folds. A paired five-minute block bootstrap places the mean log-loss gain above zero, while Signed Replenishment Asymmetry produces a strong monotonic continuation gradient. The evidence supports a microstructure mechanism on this benchmark. It does not establish executable alpha.

02 / Mechanism

The book has memory.

A depletion shock occurs when one side of a previously tight best book deteriorates without simultaneous deterioration on the opposite side. For 100 ms after the shock, the system records positive replenishment on bid and ask, exponentially weighting faster recovery more heavily.

R_bid = weighted bid additions / initial bid size
R_ask = weighted ask additions / initial ask size

LRA = (R_bid − R_ask) / (R_bid + R_ask)
SRA = shock_direction × LRA

Positive SRA means recovery rebuilds displayed liquidity in a direction supportive of the initial shock. The key test is nested: can that path improve forecasts once endpoint queue imbalance, spread and early signed price movement are already controlled?

05 / Diagnostics

Supportive replenishment maps to more persistent impact.

Continuation probability by Signed Replenishment Asymmetry decile

The monotonic spread is 28.7 percentage points. A pooled HAC diagnostic retains a positive SRA coefficient of 0.03677 after controlling for endpoint queue imbalance, spread and early signed price movement.

Regime sensitivity matters

The final 20–24 UTC block weakens sharply: conditional SRA coefficient ≈ 0.00384 with p ≈ 0.66. The mechanism is therefore reported as market-state dependent rather than a universal constant.

07 / Interpretation

A mechanism result, not a heroic Sharpe ratio.

On this fixed benchmark, recent displayed-liquidity recovery contains incremental predictive information beyond the endpoint Level-1 state.

The study does not establish persistence across dates or venues, profitability after fees and spread crossing, realistic queue position, latency-adjusted execution or superiority to deeper-book information. The highest-value extensions are multi-day replication, cross-symbol and cross-venue testing, regime interactions, depth extension and only then an execution-aware decision problem.

Conclusion

The route by which liquidity rebuilds after a depletion shock improves calibrated continuation forecasts. The strongest evidence is probabilistic and structural, not a backtested trading return.
